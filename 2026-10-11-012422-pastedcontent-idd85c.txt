  - Library: the CMOS5L library has only 89 cells and no plain flip-flop. The
    cheapest flop is 49 µm² and a NAND2 is 7 µm².
  - Current design: at the template's 60% density target there is no headroom
    for clock tree, a pin manager or any new feature.
  - Next target: once instruction memory is gone, the four FIFOs are the next
    big item. They come to 41k µm², about 28% of the SRAM-based chip.

  SRAM instead of instruction memory

  My rating is yes, it is the biggest single lever, but it needs two things
  confirmed first.

  - CMOS5L support is unconfirmed. In the PDK, sg13cmos5l_sram is a symlink to
    the SG13G2 macros, and the only SRAM silicon example is SG13G2. The
    challenge text says there are examples on "this process node". Please ask
    the organizers before depending on it.
  - Fetch timing is not yet proven. The macro read is synchronous, with 5.3 ns
    clock-to-output at the slow corner. The cycle-exact timing can be kept by
    feeding the macro the next PC, but I haven't simulated or timed that.

  Splitting one SRAM into per-engine regions works only with a dual-port macro.
  A single-port macro can't serve two engines fetching every cycle. With the 2P
  256x32 macro, the glue is just a page register per engine, about 3k µm² (an
  estimate). The shared SRAM would come to about 182k µm², 25% of nominal, with
  256 words in total. It also lets you keep several protocol images resident and
  switch between them without reloading, and it needs no ISA change. I'd choose
  between 4 × 64x16 and the shared 2P macro once we know the firmware word
  counts.

  Pin management block

  I prototyped it in synth/experiments/pin_mgr.v. It is synthesized for area
  only; I haven't simulated or integrated it.

  - Ownership version: 5.2k µm², 0.7% of nominal. Each pad has an owner, a claim
    on a pad owned by a running engine is refused, and non-owner drives are
    blocked and logged.
  - Open-drain bonus: a per-pad open-drain mode makes OUT1/SETH/SETLO I2C-safe.
    That removes the ISA change proposed in instructions.md §4.
  - Remap version: adding a pad remap, so one firmware image can run on any
    pads, costs 14.7k µm².

  I recommend adding it.
